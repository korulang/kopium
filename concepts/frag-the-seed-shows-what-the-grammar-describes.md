# The seed shows what the grammar describes

A system prompt that *describes* the wire — universal rules, the derived
grammar render, authored session facts with examples — is not enough for
every model seat. Measured on the headless db loop (live_db.k,
laguna-s-2.1:free, 2026-10): four of five turns came back empty and the
fifth answered in its native tool-call XML. Same model, same vocabulary,
same day had driven an 11-step program through the bench harness — the
delta was the bench's one `assistant` exemplar message carrying a real
program over the scope's own verbs.

The exemplar is not decoration on the prompt; it is the part of the
prompt that is itself wire. Rules tell the model the reply shape; one
assistant turn holding an actual program *shows* it, and a weak model
imitates what it can see. Per-scope — the seed must walk the vocabulary
the wall will actually accept, or it teaches a refusal on turn one.

Open: where seed-ness ends and few-shot begins is unmeasured. One turn is
what we have proof for; whether longer exemplars help or just spend
tokens nobody knows yet.
